# Bereavement Paperwork Guide — Start Here

*If you're reading this in the worst week of your life: you're not behind,
you're doing it. This is an education-only walkthrough of the paperwork that
follows a death — plain English, step by step, like a friend would explain it.
Nothing here has to be finished today. Today you only have to keep the next
small thing moving.*

---

## If you're in the first 48 hours

Skip everything else and read just two pages:

1. [The first week, gently](01-first-week.md) — what actually needs to happen
   right now (mostly: people, the funeral home, and one phone call).
2. [The call log worksheet](08-call-log.md) — print it now. People will call
   *you* starting immediately, and a one-page log keeps the facts straight
   when everything else in your head is fog.

Everyone else calling — banks, insurers, random organizations — can wait for
a response in writing. That's normal, not rude.

## What this guide is not

- **Not legal advice.** Probate, inheritance, and debt rules vary by state
  and country. This guide gives you the scaffolding and vocabulary; agencies
  and local professionals will tell you exactly what *they* need.
- **Not tax advice.** A final tax return may be involved; a pro is worth it.
- **Not grief counseling, and not medical advice.** This is admin help.
  Grief that gets heavy is real grief, not a paperwork problem — [page 7
  lists where to find more help if you need it](07-self-pacing.md#when-its-bigger-than-pacing).
- **Not urgent.** Months of lead time is normal. Deadlines that *do* exist
  are labeled as such in the text, mostly in
  [mail and benefits](05-mail-and-benefits.md).

## It's deliberately different from its neighbors

Three small guides cover three different moments, so none of them becomes a
beast:

- **caregiver-coordination-guide** — someone is *alive and needs care* now:
  sharing the load, med logs, sibling task splits.
- **digital-legacy-handbook** — *before* the worst day: preparing a digital
  life in advance (password locations, memorializing, the when-I'm-gone
  folder). If you're reading this one preventively instead, start there.
- **this guide** — *after* a death: the afterward paperwork, at bearable
  speed.

## The three working rules

1. **Verify every caller.** Nothing you're asked to do in this guide ever
   requires paying by gift card, wire, or crypto. When someone calls you,
   [hang up and dial the official number yourself](06-predatory-help-red-flags.md).
2. **Log every call.** One page. Even the useless calls — they save you the
   next useless call. ([worksheet](08-call-log.md))
3. **One small thing a day.** In shock weeks, one completed call is a full
   day's work and *counts as such*. [Page 7](07-self-pacing.md) is the
   pacing manual; it's where this rule lives.

## A promise about privacy

Nothing here needs an account, an app, or a cloud. It's paper, a binder, a
pen, and a local file or two you own. The [binder checklist](09-binder-checklist.md)
includes how to store copies safely (including what *never* goes in there),
and the [forever file](03-what-to-keep.md) chapter covers the two-places rule.
