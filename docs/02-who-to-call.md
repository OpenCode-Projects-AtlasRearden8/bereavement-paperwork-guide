# Who to Call, and in What Order

*Nobody enjoys these calls. What makes them bearable is knowing that each one
is short, scripted, and logged — and that calling "too early" simply gets you
the sentence "come back after that," which means the call cost you two
minutes, not anything real. The order below isn't law; it's just the sequence
that keeps the least breakable things safest first.*

---

## The order at a glance

Funeral providers, hospice staff, and Social Security expect these calls
constantly. Everyone else on this list handles them monthly. You are never
the first person to say this sentence on any call: *"I'm calling because a
family member passed away on [date]. I'm still learning what's needed — what
do you need from me, and in what order?"*

| # | Call | When | Why this early | Have ready |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Funeral home / crematory | Day 0–1 (usually already done) | They file the death certificate and often notify Social Security for free | Your person's full legal name, date of birth |
| 2 | Social Security (`www.ssa.gov`) | First 2–3 days | Some survivor benefits don't back-date far; they'll also say what must be returned | Date of death; their Social Security number |
| 3 | Employer(s) HR | Weeks 1 | Final paycheck, insurance through work, retirement plan next steps | Name, last day worked, "I'm [relationship]" |
| 4 | Life insurance (each policy) | Weeks 1–2 | Claims take time to pay; paperwork starts now | Policy number if found (lost policies have a missing-policy search — see [benefits](05-mail-and-benefits.md)) |
| 5 | Banks & credit unions (theirs) | Weeks 1–2 | Get each institution's *process* in writing before touching money | Whether you have any role on the account; ask "what do you need to close or transfer this?" |
| 6 | Mortgage holder OR landlord | Weeks 1–2 | Lets you ask about survivors' rights, forbearance, or lease questions calmly | Loan number from a statement, or lease |
| 7 | Utilities & phone/internet (for a house you live in) | Weeks 1–2 | Never a lapse in power, heat, or the phone number that gets mail | Account number or recent bill |
| 8 | Health insurance & Medicare | Weeks 1–2 | Coverage end dates and survivor options have windows | Medicare number (on their card) or plan card |
| 9 | Auto/home insurance agent | Weeks 2–4 | Keeps coverage right-sized; cars sitting can often switch to storage coverage | Policy numbers |
| 10 | Credit card companies (theirs) | Weeks 2–4 | They'll explain the estate process; never from your own card | The card's statement, not the card itself |
| 11 | Other government: VA, pension plans, state benefits | Weeks 2–4 | Each has its own reporting deadline and benefit to ask about | Proof of service (VA) — look, don't guess |
| 12 | USPS mail forwarding, then the sweep begins | Weeks 1–4 | Every forwarded bill is a name to add to this table | See [mail and benefits](05-mail-and-benefits.md) |
| 13 | The three credit bureaus | Weeks 2–4 | Deceased-flag and credit freeze on the file counters identity theft | [What to do — it's a short checklist](05-mail-and-benefits.md#the-credit-bureaus) |

A few notes on the order itself:

- **Why Social Security early:** short version — benefits are applied for,
  not automatic, and some have modest retroactive windows. The call itself
  takes minutes, and it stops an awkward situation: a payment arriving for
  your person after death that you then have to ask about returning.
- **Why the employer before the bank:** employer HR bundles several
  beneficiaries at once (final pay, life insurance, retirement plan) and
  often has a bereavement support person whose job is walking you through it.
- **Why the funeral-provider topics come first even in later weeks:** every
  agency downstream — DMV, banks, title offices — consumes death
  certificates, so ordering enough of them in week one (5–10+ is the common
  suggestion) is the single highest-leverage errand of the entire process.
- **No role yet? Some calls can't start without a legal estate role** (like
  executor or administrator — [glossary](10-glossary.md) has the plain
  English). Don't guess at your role. Ask each institution "what role do you
  need to hear from, and how do I show it?" and write their answer in the
  [call log](08-call-log.md). That question is how you learn the actual
  sequence for *your* family.

## Scripts — read them out loud, it helps more than you'd think

A good script has three jobs: say what happened once, decline to know things
you don't know, and get the two facts every call produces (a name, a
reference number).

> **The 30-second opener.** "Hi, my name is ____________. I'm calling about
> my ____________, who passed away on ____________. I'm not sure yet whether
> I'm your point of contact — can you tell me what you'll need from me, and
> from whom?"

> **The voicemail version.** "Hi, this is ____________ calling about
> ____________, who passed away on ____________. Please note the account and
> call me back at ____________. If it's easier to handle in writing, my
> address is ____________."

> **The third-transfer-today script.** For the moment you're deep in a phone
> maze: "Before we hang up — can I get your name, the direct number for your
> department, and a reference or case number for this call?" Then write them
> in the [call log](08-call-log.md). Three facts, every call, no exceptions.

> **The debt collector version.** When someone claims a debt and you don't yet
> understand the estate side: "Please note the account holder has passed
> away. I'm not able to discuss payments yet — I'm learning who handles the
> estate. Please send me written notice with the details, and I'll respond
> in writing." (In the U.S., you can generally ask a collector to
> communicate in writing; doing so doesn't make a debt disappear, it just
> moves things to a speed you can actually process.)

> **The verify-then-proceed rule.** If a call claims to *be* one of the
> agencies above, about a matter you didn't start: "I'm going to call you
> back on the number I have from your official site. What's your name and
> department?" Then hang up, look up the number yourself, and call. Real
> agencies expect exactly this and will tell you where to call back.

## The have-ready card

Ten facts get asked on nearly every call. Write them on one card (or
photograph this list and fill it in), and every call gets 30% shorter:

```
Their full legal name:        ______________________________
Social Security number:       ______________________________
Date and place of death:      ______________________________
Your name & relationship:     ______________________________
Your role (executor? etc.):   ______________________________
Copies ordered / on hand:     ________ certificates
Their date of birth:          ______________________________
Last known address:           ______________________________
The story of the week:        (one sentence you can repeat:
                              "We've ordered certificates and
                              notified Social Security so far.")
The log says next:            ______________________________
```

Notice the last slot — every best next step should already be written in the
[call log](08-call-log.md), so "what's next" is a lookup, not a feeling.
