# The Binder: a Checklist for One Evening

*The paperwork binder is the boring technology that wins: when any agency
asks something for the fourth time, the answer lives in a tab, not in a
frantic kitchen search. This page is the shopping list, the ten tabs, and
what goes in each. One evening of assembly (a partner with a label maker
helps; a green highlighter works fine). Print this page when the binder
is empty; check items as they land.*

---

## What to buy (or rescue from the desk drawer)

- [ ] One **3-ring binder**, 2–3 inches
- [ ] **Ten tab dividers** (numbered or write-in; both are fine)
- [ ] One plastic **sheet protector** or supplies pouch (for certificates
      and originals-in-transit)
- [ ] A pad of paper, a pen that doesn't fail, and a highlighter
- [ ] About twenty copies printed of the [call log sheet](08-call-log.md),
      or a new single-subject notebook dedicated to call notes
- [ ] Optional but useful: sticky flags, a folder-pocket for
      "waiting-on-them" papers, and one manila envelope labeled
      **COPIES WITH THE BANK NOW** (for original certificates on trips)

Storage plan while you shop: the binder lives where dinner gets cooked;
the **originals** live in a fire box, safe, or bank box (the
[two-places rule](03-what-to-keep.md)); scans live on two devices.

## The ten tabs

Each tab gets its topic's papers, newest on top. Write nothing confidential
on the tabs or spine themselves — labels like "BANK" beat full account
numbers on the outside of anything.

- [ ] **1 · Master info** — obituary printout, the family contact sheet,
      the have-ready card from [who to call](02-who-to-call.md), and the
      one-page summary of the week ("ordered 8 certificates; SSA called
      [date]").
- [ ] **2 · Certificates** — receipt of how many were ordered, the count
      still on hand, and where originals live. Update the count in
      pencil; this tab prevents the classic "we're out" panic.
- [ ] **3 · Identity & records** — copies (only!) of ID, Social Security
      card, birth and marriage certificates. Tell yourself plainly on this
      tab's note: "originals in the fire box."
- [ ] **4 · Funeral & prepaid plan** — the contract, the receipt of what
      was paid, the prepaid plan booklet, correspondence.
- [ ] **5 · Government: Social Security, Medicare, VA** — every letter
      from each agency, application copies, award or denial letters
      (these become [forever-file residents](03-what-to-keep.md)).
- [ ] **6 · Employer & retirement** — HR correspondence, beneficiary
      confirmations, COBRA or plan paperwork.
- [ ] **7 · Insurance** — one sub-pile per policy (life, health, home,
      auto): claims started, confirmation numbers, cashed-payment records.
- [ ] **8 · Banks, cards & debts** — closure letters, zero-balance
      statements, written responses from creditors, the
      [don't-pay-yet notes](05-mail-and-benefits.md#the-dont-pay-yet-principle).
- [ ] **9 · House, vehicles & titles** — utility transfers, lease or
      mortgage letters, title transfer papers, lien releases.
- [ ] **10 · Subscriptions & the log** — the
      [subscriptions tracker](04-accounts-and-subscriptions.md) printouts
      or the CSV printed, cancellation confirmations, and the three-ring
      home of the [call log](08-call-log.md). The "done" drawer.

## Habits that keep the binder earning its shelf space

- **Inbox → binder in one step.** When papers arrive in the mail, they go
  into the tab they belong to (or a holding pocket, sorted on the
  low-power day menu). No third pile ever forms.
- **Pencil for status; pen for facts.** Claims, account statuses, and
  "next steps" change; names and dates don't.
- **The Sunday ten.** Ten minutes on the least-griefy day of the week:
  file the week's papers, check next steps in the log, glance for anything
  dated. That's the whole maintenance plan.
- **One year in**, the binder becomes the *forever file*: most tabs
  graduate to the box, and four or five (certificates, closing letters,
  tax confirmations, the log) move to long-term keeping
  ([keep-for table](03-what-to-keep.md)).

## If you'd rather not use paper at all

The binder is the *default* because paper survives shock-brain, power
outages, and "where were we" months. But everything in this guide works as
plain local files too — folders instead of tabs, scan photos instead of
copies, the CSV worksheets instead of printed sheets. If a family member
wants to be the spreadsheet sibling, split the tabs between you; the
[binder concept](#the-ten-tabs) survives any software. Only two rules
transfer unchanged: copies in two places, and secrets (passwords, full
numbers beyond the last four digits) never written on labels, files, or
folders where a stranger's eyes might land.
