# The First Week, Gently

*This week is for people, not paperwork. The paperwork in this guide takes
months and does not punish you for starting slowly. What follows is the short
list that actually matters in the first days, then a week-one checklist, then
— just as important — a list of things you're allowed to not do yet.*

---

## The first days are mostly handled by professionals

What must happen on day one depends on where it happened. None of these need
you to know anything; the professionals on the other end of these calls walk
you through their part:

- **At home, under hospice care:** call the hospice's 24-hour number first —
  not 911. Hospice staff are trained for exactly this and will tell you who
  to call and what to do while you wait. They usually arrange for the funeral
  home to come.
- **At home, unexpected:** call 911 (or your local emergency number). The
  responders will handle the rest of that day and tell you what happens next.
  Nothing here is on you.
- **In a hospital, nursing home, or care facility:** the staff will typically
  ask if there's a funeral home to call, and a social worker or chaplain can
  help with everything that follows. You don't need to have answers ready.

One small comfort: the medical side — pronouncing, the death certificate's
medical part, transport — is handled by the hospice, hospital, or medical
examiner's office. The funeral home you choose helps file the
[certificates](10-glossary.md) among their first tasks.

## People first

- **Pick a spokesperson.** Whoever wants to, or whoever is calmest. Their
  whole job is: "Thank you — we're not making plans yet; we'll share
  arrangements when they're set" said kindly, seventeen times, so you don't
  have to.
- **Dependents and pets need today's help.** Kids (tell them the truth in
  small pieces; you don't have to say everything at once), anyone the person
  was caring for, and the animals. If someone lived alone, this is the day a
  neighbor friend checks the house.
- **Secure the routine things of the house.** Doors locked, plants and mail,
  cars moved off street parking if the home will sit empty for a while. That's
  the whole list — deep sorting doesn't belong to this week.

## The "not yet" list

Here's permission, in writing. None of this needs to happen this week —
most of it shouldn't:

- Don't close bank accounts or call everyone back on statements you found.
  ([That's the monthly version of this guide.](04-accounts-and-subscriptions.md))
- Don't pay their big bills, and definitely don't pay them with your own
  personal money "just to be safe." Nobody should pay a debt from their own
  pocket until they understand where it's supposed to be paid from.
- Don't sign anything on the spot — funeral extras below are the exception
  that earns your trust, and even there you can pause everything except what's
  needed for the service.
- Don't sell, give away, or clear-out anything yet. Things you can't see the
  grief in today look different in sixty days.
- Don't reply to every text tonight. "Thank you; more soon" is a text.

## The week-one checklist

A real checklist, but again: one line a day is a fine pace.

- [ ] Choose a funeral home or crematory if not already done; ask them two
      things: **how many certified death certificates should we order?**
      (commonly suggested: 5–10+, because extra ones are cheap now and slow
      later) and **do you file the Social Security notification for us?**
      (most do, at no charge.)
- [ ] Check for **prepaid funeral plans** — funeral homes, some unions and
      churches, some states' plans, and some life-insurance policies include
      them. Search your person's files for the words "pre-need," "prepaid,"
      or a plan booklet.
- [ ] Call **Social Security** — or confirm the funeral home did. It's the
      one call with a mostly-earliest start for benefits, and they answer
      politely on a national number you can find at `www.ssa.gov`. Ask plainly:
      *"Is anyone in this family eligible for anything?"*
- [ ] Call the **employer's HR department** (current or most recent job):
      final paycheck, health insurance timeline, life insurance through work,
      retirement account beneficiary forms. HR handles these calls weekly;
      you won't be the first.
- [ ] Start a **single folder** for every paper that arrives, even junk —
      a shoebox is a real filing system for now. Sort later. ([The binder comes
      in a few weeks.](09-binder-checklist.md))
- [ ] Begin the [call log](08-call-log.md) — one line per call, even the
      calls that went nowhere.
- [ ] Ask the funeral home for **2 extra copies of any paperwork** they give
      you; keep the originals in the folder ([later, the forever file holds
      them](03-what-to-keep.md)).
- [ ] If the home will sit empty, arrange a friend's **walk-through check**
      every day or two. Lock up any documents a stranger shouldn't see
      (statements, mail, IDs, pills to be disposed of properly later).
- [ ] Pick **one date this week** that you will not do paperwork on. Write
      it down. That day is protected like an appointment.
