# Call Log Worksheet

*One page, two minutes per call, no exceptions — including the calls that
go nowhere. Especially those: the point of this sheet is that you never
have to wonder "did I already call the electric company?" or "what was
that reference number?" again. In shock-brain weeks, this page is your
external memory. Print several.*

---

## How to use it

For **every** call, inbound or outbound:

1. Fill a row. All eight columns is ideal; three (date, who, next step) is
   enough to save the day.
2. Get and write down the **three facts that protect you**: the agent's
   name, the reference or case number, and the fill-in direct number.
3. When the call ends, write the **next step and its date** before you hang
   up — "they'll mail it" becomes "letter due [date]; call if
   silent."
4. Mark the last column **done** only when written confirmation exists
   (a letter, an email, or the confirmation number itself).
5. Twenty minutes later, that's a real day. The pacing rules live in
   [self-pacing](07-self-pacing.md); this sheet lives in the
   [binder's log tab](09-binder-checklist.md).A fillable spreadsheet version with the same columns lives at
**[worksheets/call-log.csv](../worksheets/call-log.csv)** — open it in any
spreadsheet app on your own machine.

## Callbacks and follow-ups that belong here too

- "They said they'd call me back" — a row with the date they promised, and
  today's date as the reminder.
- "I need to find out X first" — a row: the question, who to ask about it.
- Mail received that demanded a response — the envelope's company is the
  "who" column; the sweep of the mail lives in
  [mail and benefits](05-mail-and-benefits.md).
- Any call where something felt off — write down the number that called
  you and what they wanted. That row feeds the
  [red flags chapter](06-predatory-help-red-flags.md) directly.
