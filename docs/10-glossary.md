# Glossary

*Every word in this guide that shows up on a phone call or a form,
translated to plain English first. Reading these before the calls is
free confidence: the same vocabulary is yours now. (This is education,
not a legal reference — states use slightly different words for some of
these concepts.)*

---

| Term | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| Administrator | The court-appointed person who handles an estate when there's no will naming anyone (or no will at all). Same job as an executor; different name. |
| Affidavit | A written statement sworn to be true, usually signed in front of a notary. Small paperwork, big doors. |
| Beneficiary | The person (or people) named on an account, insurance policy, or retirement plan to receive the money or benefit. Beneficiary forms usually outrank the will. |
| Certified copy (death certificate) | An official copy with seals and signatures, printed by a government office — the version agencies actually accept. Photocopies of photocopies are not these. |
| Claim | A formal request to an insurance company to pay a benefit. Life insurance requires a claim; "canceling" a life policy is a different thing entirely. |
| Creditor | Any person or company the deceased person owed money to. |
| Decedent | Formal word for the person who died. Grief writers often just say "your person." |
| Deed | The ownership record for a house or land, usually recorded with a county office. |
| Estate | Everything the person left behind: money, property, belongings, and debts too — treated as a sort of temporary accounting entity that debts get paid from. |
| Estate bank account | An account opened for an estate (usually by the executor, with court letters) used to pay the estate's legitimate bills. Not anyone's personal account. |
| Executor / executrix | The person named in a will to carry it out. Modern courts mostly say **personal representative** (see next row). |
| Letters of administration | The court paper naming an administrator when there's no will. |
| Letters testamentary | The court paper that says an executor is officially allowed to act for the estate. This small-looking document is what banks ask for. |
| Lump-sum death payment | A one-time Social Security payment (commonly $255) to an eligible surviving spouse or child. Applied-for, not automatic. |
| Mail forwarding (deceased) | The post office's specific process for rerouting a deceased person's mail — usually an in-person stop with proof. |
| Next of kin | The closest living family member(s) by law. Which relative counts, and in what order, varies by state. |
| Obituary | The public death announcement — whose content (times, addresses of services) deserves a safety pause. See [red flags](06-predatory-help-red-flags.md). |
| Personal representative | The umbrella modern term: whoever the court empowers to settle an estate — executor, administrator, or the like. |
| Power of attorney (POA) | Authority to act for someone **while they're alive**. It **ends the moment the person dies** — a new legal role (executor/personal representative) is what steps in. This surprises almost everyone. |
| Prepaid funeral plan / pre-need | Funeral arrangements paid in advance. Find the paperwork early; most states protect the money in specific ways. |
| Probate | The court process that officially recognizes a death, settles debts, and transfers what's left according to the will (or state rules). Small estates often get simplified paths — that's worth asking about. |
| Probate loan / inheritance advance | A private company advancing money against an inheritance, for a fee. Legal, sometimes useful, never something to decide under pressure. |
| Proof-of-death letter | An institution's own letter confirming it was notified of the death. Cheap insurance against re-proving things; keep for good. |
| Secured debt | A debt tied to a specific thing (house, car) that the lender can repossess if unpaid. Mortgages and car loans. |
| Small-estate affidavit | A shorter form-based path for modest estates in many states, skipping full probate. The court clerk's office can say whether it applies. |
| Social Security statement | SSA's records of a person's earnings history — helpful for estimating survivor benefits. |
| Survivor benefits | Ongoing Social Security (and sometimes pension) payments to eligible family members. Applied-for; windows matter; ask plainly. |
| Unclaimed property | Money or valuables in forgotten accounts, held by the state until claimed. Each state has a free official search; fees are a red flag. |
| Unsecured debt | A debt with nothing tied to it — most credit cards, medical bills, personal loans. |
| Vehicle title transfer | The formal change of a car's ownership record at the state DMV after a death (certificates usually required). |
| Will | The document saying who should get what. It doesn't move money by itself — it tells the court and executor what to do. |
